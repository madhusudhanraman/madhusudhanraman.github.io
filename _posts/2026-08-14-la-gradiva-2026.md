_La Gradiva_ takes its name from an early twentieth century novella
titled _Gradiva: A Pompeiian Fancy_ (1902) by Wilhelm Jensen. Like its
namesake, the bulk of the action in this film takes place amid the
ruins of Pompei, where a teacher named Ms. Mercier (sharply and
sympathetically played by Antonia Buresi) chaperones a field trip of
French high-school students.

The students --- in many delightful and enjoyably written scenes that
feature them conversing with each other, their teachers, or
interacting with the world around them --- are eminently recognisable;
over the past few years, I have taught groups of students similarly
constituted, each among them bursting with a youthful energy,
intelligent, irreverent, playful, some more given to brooding than
others, and yet not often sure of themselves and the unsteady first
steps they take in adult life and love. The scenes where Mercier drags
her class kicking and screaming towards an appreciation of geology,
history, and art were a delight to watch, perhaps because they so
reminded me of my own experiences.

James (Capellier-Audat) is a charmer, and shares a deep friendship
with Toni (Quignard), and the nature of this friendship forming a
source of tension between the two. For Toni, this field trip is a
homecoming: he reveals early in the film that his grandmother was a
maid to an aristocratic Italian family, and it was there that she met
and fell in love with his grandfather. Toni's mother has led him to
believe that his grandfather perished soon after she was conceived,
crushed under the rubble that the castle was reduced to during an
earthquake. Toni chances upon the castle that ought to have been
destroyed, and Suzanne (Gerin), a quiet classmate given to a gloomy
solitude, accompanies him to investigate. There, aided by a rather
cruel Suzanne, Toni comes to question the story he was told. From then
on, to borrow from Jensen's novella, "he was moved by an indefinite
feeling that everything was growing darker and darker about and within
him."

Unlike Jensen's novella, the film does not end happily. It is
nevertheless a delicate, charming, and ultimately sublime film.
